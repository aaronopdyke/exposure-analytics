Methodology

This site compares up to seven building-exposure datasets, country by country, on a single map aggregated to World Bank Official Boundaries Admin-1 units. The methodology below is the same for every country; the concrete numbers on this page are filled in for the country selected in the header — currently … — and change when you switch. Two are top-down models (GAR15, GIRI), two are releases of a bottom-up engineering exposure model (GEM v2023.1.1 and v2026.0.0), and three are building-by-building maps (Overture, Global Building Atlas, Microsoft). They answer subtly different questions, so differences between them are expected and informative — and the vintage analysis below separates differences that are just data age from differences that are fundamental.

The models publish USD of different base years, so every monetary figure on this site is converted to constant latest-year US$ with the World Bank WDI US GDP deflator (NY.GDP.DEFL.ZS, country USA; target year and per-model factors are recorded per country in data/<ISO>/meta.json):

Does data age explain the gaps? (vintage analysis)

Even in constant dollars the datasets describe the building stock of different years — GAR15's inputs are ~2011, GIRI's ~2020, GBA's ~2023, GEM v2026's 2025. The explorer's "Vintage adjustment" toggle (and the deck's vintage slides) grow each dataset from its own data vintage to the latest year, using two data-driven rates instead of a guessed constant:

Values follow the actual stock path 1995–2020 and extrapolate at the trailing-10-year CAGR beyond it. Why toggle it? To test whether a gap between two models is just data age. By default the site shows each model's own base-year values; switching to "grown" answers "what would this dataset say about today's stock if its method were left unchanged?". If age were the explanation, the grown figures would converge. Models whose data already reflects the latest year (GEM v2026, Overture) have a growth factor of ×1.00 and do not move.

Sanity check — does the value/floor wedge match GEM's own repricing? The two rates imply the stock's average real value per m² rises ≈…/yr. GEM's releases test this independently (this country): implied national unit cost (replacement value ÷ floor area) went from …/m² (2021 US$, v2023) to …/m² (2024 US$, v2026) — … nominal, or ≈…/yr in real terms after the US deflator (×… over 2021→2024). Where the two agree, GEM's own repricing corroborates the value path; where they diverge, GEM re-modelled the stock between releases rather than only repricing it. (Caveat: 2021–2024 spans the post-COVID construction-cost spike, so the long-run wedge is likely somewhat smaller; "real" is net of general inflation, so the wedge includes construction costs outpacing the deflator as well as quality/composition upgrading.)

Estimated replacement values for the footprint datasets (GEM UCC)

Overture, GBA and Microsoft map buildings but carry no monetary value. Their "value" layers here are estimated replacement values:

estimated replacement value = floor area × ADM1 unit construction cost (UCC)

UCC (USD/m², 2024 US$) comes from the UCC-database compilation of the GEM exposure model — BLDG_REPL_COST_USD / TOTAL_AREA_SQM per GEM admin-1, all occupancies combined (this country: …/m² across units). It excludes contents. GEM admin-1 UCCs are mapped to the display units via the region matching below; a unit that pools several GEM regions uses their area-weighted UCC, and a unit with no GEM region the national one.

Floor area and storey estimation

GEM reports floor area directly. For the building maps it is derived:

  • GBA has modelled heights for nearly every building: floor area = footprint × storeys via a three-tier storey height (<18 m: 3.0 m, <60 m: 3.25 m, else 3.5 m per storey); missing heights count as one storey.
  • Overture and Microsoft are close to footprint-only datasets — Overture's num_floors/height are rarely populated and Microsoft's height field is mostly −1 (this country: attributes cover … of Overture footprint area). Where attributes exist they are used; for the remainder, storeys are estimated with a GBA-calibrated multiplier: the GBA floor-area/footprint ratio computed per ADM1 unit and urban/rural class, applied to each dataset's unattributed footprint. This replaces the earlier 1-storey assumption with the local storey profile of the one source that has heights.

How sensitive are totals to the storey rules? The build recomputes the footprint models under four alternative rules — integer storeys rounded down (min 1), rounded up, and the storey divisor shifted ±0.25 m — and records the envelope in each country's meta.json (sensitivity block). For this country the extreme rules move footprint-model totals by roughly … (integer storeys rounded down) to … (rounded up), with the divisor shifts contributing … — against a … spread between the native-value models.

Boundaries: one display standard

All comparisons are displayed on WB Official Boundaries Admin-1. Two situations need an explicit policy.

Non-determined legal status areas (Morocco). WB draws Morocco ending at 27.67°N and carries Western Sahara only as an ADM0 polygon (ISO ESH, "non-determined legal status area") with no ADM1 subdivisions. Maps here therefore show Western Sahara as one whole, hatched unit (ESH000) appended to the Morocco display — never subdivided and never merged into a member state. A consequence worth knowing: WB retains a small sliver of Laâyoune-Sakia al Hamra north of 27.67°N (MAR007), which is why that unit shows very small totals in every dataset.

Countries without Admin-1 subdivisions (Marshall Islands). WB carries some small island states as a single Admin-1 unit. A one-unit map says nothing, so the WB polygon — the official coastline — is split into atolls using the atoll names of the SPC / OCHA Common Operational Dataset (COD-AB): every islet goes to its nearest named atoll, which also folds the uninhabited atolls into the municipality that customarily administers them. Each atoll is then drawn, and every dataset aggregated, as an envelope around its islets (lagoon included): islets a few hundred metres wide would be invisible on a country map and would miss the 5 km cells of the gridded models.

Reconciling ADM1 where boundary sets differ

The models do not share an admin-1 geography, so each is brought onto the WB units with a method suited to its native form:

ModelNative geographyReconciliation to WB ADM1
GAR15Grid points, per-country files Point-in-polygon; points whose centres fall just offshore (1 km coastal cells) recovered by a bounded nearest join (≤0.1°). For Morocco the separate ESH file supplies ESH000. Assignment loss ≈ 0%.
GIRIGlobal 5 km raster Each cell assigned wholly to the WB unit it overlaps most (largest-intersection rule) — keeps coastal cells, avoids double counting, and deliberately has no nearest-neighbour fallback so cells from neighbouring countries cannot leak in.
GEM (both releases)Own ADM1 (official regions, departments or atolls) Region matching: each GEM region is matched to its display unit by normalised region name (accents and punctuation ignored, with a short alias list where names genuinely differ); the build stops if any region is left unmatched. Morocco uses a curated table instead, because the boundary sets differ: by region codes for v2026 (MA-01…MA-12) and names for v2023; ten regions map 1:1 and GEM's two southern regions, lying almost wholly south of the WB 27.67°N line, are assigned to ESH000, so the WB MAR007 sliver shows no GEM value.
Overture / GBA / MicrosoftIndividual buildings Building centroid-in-polygon, evaluated inside DuckDB against the full-resolution display units; footprint areas are geodesic (WGS84 ellipsoid).

Urban / rural classification

What "urban/rural exists for residential only" means for GEM v2026: GEM publishes its summary rows as ADM1 × occupancy. For residential buildings each row is tagged URBAN or RURAL; for commercial and industrial buildings GEM publishes only totals with no settlement tag. So a "GEM urban" figure on this site is the urban share of GEM's residential stock only (commercial/industrial value still appears in totals and in the res/com/ind breakdown chart). For some countries v2026 carries no settlement tags at all, and the v2023 release never does.

ModelHow the split is obtained
GAR15Native: urban (tot_cu) and rural (tot_cr) capital columns, all sectors.
GEM v2026Native but residential only (see above); v2023 has none.
Overture, GBA, MicrosoftAssigned by this project: each building's 1 km Mollweide grid cell is looked up in GHSL Degree of Urbanisation (DEGURBA) E2020, R2025A; classes 21/22/23/30 (suburban through urban centre) count as urban, classes 11–13 (and water) as rural.
GIRINot split — GIRI publishes residential / non-residential layers instead, shown as sub-layers in single-model mode and in the breakdown chart.

Comparability caveats

  • Value definitions differ: GAR15 and GIRI start from national produced capital, which covers machinery and equipment as well as structures and carries a 24% World Bank mark-up for urban land. GAR15 assigns all of it to buildings, infrastructure included; GIRI subtracts a separate infrastructure stock. Both are shown here divided by 1.24 to remove the land mark-up — the one part that can be taken out — and so still overstate buildings. GEM is engineering replacement cost; Overture/GBA/Microsoft are estimated replacement values (floor area × UCC). None equals market or insured value. GEM's contents are excluded — so its figures here are lower than GEM's own published country totals, which include a contents column (this country, GEM v2026: … structures shown here vs … incl. … contents on GEM's site).
  • Vintages differ even after price-year alignment — see the vintage analysis above for how much that explains (little, between methods).
  • Completeness differs fundamentally: the footprint datasets count mapped buildings; the models distribute national aggregates, so they include what mapping misses (and vice versa).
  • All zonal work uses centroid / largest-overlap assignment as above; totals exclude features outside the WB coastline generalisation.
  • Access: the site is open — the landing page is just the country selection, and the files under data/ and assets/ (aggregated ADM1 statistics from public datasets) are fetchable by direct URL.
